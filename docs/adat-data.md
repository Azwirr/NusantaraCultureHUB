# Adat and customary ceremonies: verified research notes

Research date: 24 October 2026. Status: research notes, not published content.

## How these were checked

- Each of the nine entries was found by web search, then two different websites were opened and read.
- A fact is listed as **confirmed** when both sites say it. Facts from one site only are marked **(one source)**.
- Where the two sites disagree, both versions are shown under **Conflicts**. Nothing was picked silently.
- Claims that the sites did not support were dropped. Those are listed under **Not confirmed** so they are not reused by mistake.
- No picture or media was searched for here. Pictures are for Codex.
- Nothing in this file is in the database. The project has no table for ceremonies yet (see [Next steps](#next-steps)).

Source abbreviations used below: **detik** = detik.com, **Kompas** = kompas.com, **Liputan6** = liputan6.com, **GNFI** = goodnewsfromindonesia.id.

## 1. Pasola (Sumba Barat, NTT)

**What it is:** A mounted contest held once a year at the start of the rice planting season. Horsemen from two groups throw wooden spears at each other. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Held in February in Kecamatan Lamboya, and in March in Wanokaka, Laboya Barat and Gaura. The exact day is set by the rato (traditional leaders) from moon phases and natural signs. **(confirmed)**
- For about a month before, residents keep taboos, such as no parties and no building houses. **(confirmed)**
- Begins with the nyale ceremony: sea worms appear on the beaches at the full moon. Plump worms are a sign of luck and a good harvest, and Pasola can only be held after they are collected. **(detik; Liputan6 names the harvest link)**
- Linked to the Marapu belief: thanks for prosperity and a good harvest, and a request for forgiveness. **(Liputan6; detik does not mention Marapu)**
- A legend of three brothers and a wife who eloped. The brothers agreed to hold Pasola as a sign of peace. **(detik)**
- Blood spilled in the contest is seen as a sign of fertility. A death is seen as a sign that customary rules were broken. **(detik; Liputan6 does not mention deaths)**

**Conflicts**
- Spear size: detik says about 1.5 cm in diameter with blunt tips. Liputan6 says about two metres long. The sources do not agree; leave the size out of any published text until it is checked.

**Sources:** detik, Husna Putri Maharani, 9 May 2024 (detikBali). Liputan6, Resla Aknaita Chak, 13 October 2022.

## 2. Ngaben (Bali, Balinese Hindu)

**What it is:** A cremation ceremony. It frees the soul from worldly ties and returns the body's five elements to their source. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Five elements (Panca Maha Bhuta): pertiwi (solid), apah (liquid), teja (heat), bayu (wind), akasa (space). **(confirmed)**
- Opening stages: ngulapin (asking permission at the Pura Dalem) and meseh lawang (a rite at a crossroads or cemetery edge). **(Kompas only; the article says the rest of the process is on later pages)**
- The fire is charged first with a mantra, then burns the body. A traditional pyre takes 4 to 6 hours with firewood. A gas crematorium takes 1 to 1.5 hours. Ashes go into a young coconut and are scattered in the sea. **(GNFI only)**
- The vessel (patulangan) is shaped like an animal or mythical creature. The tiered tower (bade) carries the body, and higher castes usually use it. **(GNFI only)**

**Not confirmed**
- "Bull-shaped container" (Petulangan). GNFI describes an animal or mythical shape, not a bull. Do not write "bull" without a source that says so.
- The types of Ngaben. The Kompas title promises them, but the text read did not include them.

**Sources:** Kompas, Verelladevanka Adryamarthanino, 22 September 2021. GNFI, 20 June 2021 (author listed as Iip M. Aditiya).

## 3. Tiwah (Dayak Ngaju, Central Kalimantan)

**What it is:** A death ritual of the Dayak Ngaju, practised under the Kaharingan faith. It sends the dead person's spirit to the spirit world and clears misfortune from the family. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Duration: 3, 4, 7 or 40 days, depending on scale. **(detik)** The main ceremony runs from 3 days to a month. **(Kompas)**
- Day 4: the bones are collected from the grave, cleaned, wrapped in cloth and carried to the Tiwah hall. Day 7: placed in a sandung (bone house) or pantar. **(detik; Kompas also mentions the sandung)**
- Sapundu: a carved ironwood post where sacrificial animals are tied. **(both)**
- Animals: chicken, pig, cow or buffalo. **(detik)** A buffalo is tied at the sangkaraya structure. **(Kompas)**
- Obligation: the spirit must be sent to its place. The belief is that a spirit not sent through Tiwah stays in this world. **(Kompas only)**
- Cost: at least Rp 50 to 100 million. A large Tiwah also shows family wealth, and several families can share the cost. **(Kompas only)**

**Conflicts**
- Name of the spirit world: detik says **Lewu Tatau**. Kompas says **Lewu Liaw**. Both are Kaharingan names; the source for each should be cited, not merged.

**Sources:** detik, Bayu Ardi Isnanto, 20 September 2025 (detikKalimantan). Kompas, 1 March 2022 (regional; editor William Ciputra).

## 4. Sekaten (Yogyakarta and Surakarta, Java)

**What it is:** A Javanese ceremony that marks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad (Maulid Nabi), held by the royal courts. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Dates: from 5 to 11 Mulud (Rabi'ul Awal), with Grebeg Mulud on 12 Mulud. **(both)**
- Sunan Kalijaga is credited with starting it: he used gamelan to draw crowds, then taught the creed. **(both)**
- Grebeg Mulud on the 12th: a gunungan (cone of rice, food and fruit offerings) is carried from the palace to the mosque and given out. People believe it brings blessing. **(both)**
- The gamelan is played for six days, with breaks for prayer, and not on Friday night. **(detikJogja)**

**Conflicts**
- Gamelan names: detikJogja gives Kanjeng Kiai Gunturmadu and Kanjeng Kiai Nagawilaga. Kompas gives Kyai Nogowilogo and Kyai Guntur Madu. Pick one source for any name and cite it.
- Playing times: detikJogja says 3 PM to 11 PM. Kompas says a midnight parade to the mosque. Not used in the summary.

**Sources:** detikJogja, Galardialga Kustanto, 22 September 2023. Kompas (Skola section), Ani Rachman, 7 February 2023.

## 5. Seren Taun (Sunda, West Java)

**What it is:** A Sundanese harvest thanksgiving. The name joins *seren* (to hand over) and *taun* (year), a handover of one year's harvest to the next. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Honours Nyi Pohaci Sanghyang Sri (Dewi Sri), the rice goddess. **(both)**
- Pare Ambu (mother rice) and Pare Abah (father rice) represent the union of female and male. Mother rice is kept under white cloth, father rice under black. **(GNFI only)**
- In Cigugur, Kuningan, the main event falls on 22 Rayagung (the Sundanese name for Dzulhijjah). The celebration starts with Damar Sewu. **(detik only)**
- Still practised by Kasepuhan communities, such as Ciptagelar and Banten Kidul, and in Kanekes (Baduy), Kampung Naga and Cigugur. **(detik only)**
- In Kampung Urug, Bogor, cooked rice is buried as an offering to the spirits of the soil. **(detik only)**
- Said to date back to the Pajajaran kingdom. **(detik; GNFI mentions a Pajajaran variant held every eight years)**

**Conflicts**
- Timing: GNFI says it is held every year. detik says practice varies by community. Both can be true; state the variation.

**Sources:** GNFI, Siti Padilah, 4 November 2023. detik Jabar, Dian Nugraha Ramdani, 14 May 2025.

## 6. Yadnya Kasada (Tengger, Mount Bromo, East Java)

**What it is:** The Tengger people's yearly offering ceremony at Bromo, giving thanks to Sang Hyang Widhi and honouring ancestors. Farm produce, crops and livestock are cast into the crater. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Held on the 15th day of the 12th month (Kasada) of the Tengger calendar. **(detik only)**
- The 2026 ceremony took place in the early hours of 1 June 2026 at Pura Luhur Poten. **(Kompas only)**
- The main rite is the lelabuhan procession from Poten to the crater, with mantras and a village feast (slametan) at the end. **(detik only)**
- Legend: a childless couple prayed on the mountain and were promised children, on condition that the last child be offered to the crater. The youngest child then vanished and asked for offerings every year. **(detik; Kompas gives the same myth in outline)**
- The name Tengger is said to join parts of the couple's names: teng (Anteng) and ger (Seger). **(Kompas only)**

**Conflicts**
- Names in the legend: detik gives **Roro Anteng** and **Joko Seger** (the youngest child **Raden Usuma**, caption "Kesuma"). Kompas gives **Rara Anteng** and **Jaka Seger**. Cite one and say which.

**Sources:** Kompas travel, Ella Triana, 3 June 2026. detik Jatim, Ardian Dwi Kurnia, 19 June 2024.

## 7. Mapalus (Minahasa, North Sulawesi)

**What it is:** Mutual cooperation groups of the Minahasa people, practised across North Sulawesi. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Philosophy Sitou Timou Tumou Tou (live and work together so that others may live). **(GNFI only)**
- Forms: farming (the original form), weddings and celebrations, and funerals organised through village or family associations. **(GNFI only)**
- Happy and sad occasions are both covered; the work is done in turns, with labour and materials. **(Kompas)**
- Rules: members who are given a task must attend. Members who miss without reason can be excluded. A sick member pays an agreed sum or finds a replacement. **(GNFI only)**

**Not confirmed**
- "Palus" and a giving-back principle. Neither source explains the word *palus*. Do not write it as a meaning until a source does.

**Sources:** Kompas Regional, Dini Daniswari, 17 November 2022. GNFI, Afdal Hasan, 29 December 2022.

## 8. Bakar Batu (Papua)

**What it is:** A Papuan earth-oven feast, a thanksgiving to God, held for celebrations, welcoming guests and making peace. Stones are heated until they glow, and the food is cooked on them. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Occasions: births, weddings, installing a chief, before going to war (Kompas); welcoming guests, weddings, births, honouring ancestors and restoring peace (Liputan6). **(both, with different lists)**
- Process: men gather stones, dig a pit and heat the stones. Women prepare food such as sweet potato, cassava, vegetables and meat, wrapped in banana or pandan leaves. Layers of stone, leaves and food go into the pit, covered with soil for several hours. **(Liputan6 only; Kompas says the stones are used but does not describe the pit)**
- Groups named: Dani, Lani and Mee. **(Liputan6 only)**
- Meat: historically pork in the highlands. Many communities now use goat, cattle or chicken out of respect for other beliefs. **(Kompas; Liputan6 says pork or chicken depending on custom)**
- Shared equally regardless of status; when former rivals share a feast, it marks reconciliation. **(Liputan6 only)**

**Sources:** Kompas Regional, 4 August 2023 (no named writer). Liputan6, Panji Prayitno and Belvana Fasya Saad, 21 March 2025.

## 9. Ma'nene (Toraja, South Sulawesi)

**What it is:** A Toraja ritual to honour ancestors. The families take the bodies from the tomb, clean them and put on new clothes. **(confirmed)**

**Key facts**
- Timing: once every three years now, after harvest, decided by customary deliberation (Kombongan Kalua). It used to be yearly. The reasons given are cost and family members who live far away. **(both: detik says yearly before, three-yearly now; Kompas says three-yearly after harvest)**
- Name: Kompas gives the meaning "wrapping again" (membungkus kembali), citing Ahmad Suthami Putra (2024). **(Kompas only)**
- Process: an opening prayer in old Toraja; pigs and buffalo are sacrificed, with pigs matching the number of mummies in the tomb; the tomb is opened with the Pa'tene rite; the bodies are sunned for three days to a week, cleaned and re-dressed; the ritual ends with Ma'sisemba, a leg-fighting contest. **(detik only)**
- Origin: a legend of a hunter, Pong Rumase, whose remains were found and honoured. **(detik only)**

**Sources:** Kompas, Silmi Nurul Utami, 27 August 2025. detik Sulsel, Rachmat Ariadi, 3 September 2022.
